# Same CRM, twelve countries, twelve different realities

*Field note on the moment a global rollout is "done" and value creation has barely begun — and how to score the gap before it becomes a boardroom fight.*

---

## The uneven-rollout problem

Every multi-region enterprise has the same moment. The CRM platform is live across a dozen markets. The headquarters rollout dashboard is green. And somewhere in the org, one country's service team is quietly running conversations end-to-end with the platform doing most of the work, while another country's team is opening three browser tabs to close a single case.

Same software. Same templates. Same global standard org.

The distance between those two entities is not the software's fault. It's the accumulated drift of local process, data quality, dealer incentives, channel choices, and staffing decisions made over five or six years. And unless you can *measure* that distance, you can't argue about what to do next. Everyone just keeps repeating that the rollout is done — because on paper, it is.

## What I actually did

Working with the Hyundai Motor Group global CRM team, I ran a Service Cloud Maturity Assessment across more than a dozen country entities on the same underlying platform — Korea, North America, India, several European markets, Australia, Brazil, Southeast Asia.

Not a slideware assessment. A rubric one.

Each entity was scored on the same five dimensions:

- **Channel Strategy** — is the customer met on every relevant channel, and are they routed by intent?
- **Information Architecture** — is self-service organized by product, or by the actual verbs customers use?
- **Search Experience** — does the search bar return a link list, an AI-generated answer, or an actionable widget?
- **Service Console Maturity** — do agents pivot between systems by hand, or does the console surface AI-generated summaries and next-best-actions?
- **Agentic Self-Service** — does the customer get an *answer*, or an *action* that resolves the issue autonomously?

Each dimension used the same 1–5 rubric (Minimal → Emerging → Practicing → Optimised → Leading) and had to be grounded in evidence — a forensic audit of the entity's live customer-facing surfaces (the actual Contact Us page, the actual help center, the actual search bar), cross-checked against CRM data on their real platform footprint. Nobody's opinion. Nobody's slide claim. What the customer, on their phone, would encounter today.

## What the portfolio view revealed

Reading a dozen entity scorecards side by side is a fundamentally different experience from reading one.

The gap between the highest-scoring and lowest-scoring entity was wider than any single stakeholder had believed — and the pattern was consistent. Three dimensions bottlenecked almost every trailing entity: data integration, agent-autonomous handling, and end-to-end performance measurement. That's not a software problem. That's an operating-model problem, shared across the group.

Which also meant the fix wasn't "buy more platform." It was: replicate what the leading entity already does — because it's already inside the same license, on the same org, using the same connectors. The playbook exists. It's just not distributed.

That reframes the boardroom conversation completely. The question is no longer *"is the rollout done?"* (it is). It becomes *"what will take each entity to the next level, and where does the group get the biggest return for the least new investment?"*

That's the shift from Roll-out to ROI.

## Why the rubric matters more than the score

Anyone can guess at a maturity score. The value isn't the number — it's that the number is defensible.

When a regional GM disagrees with "Emerging," the rubric can point at the live audit: *your public search bar returned five link cards, not an answer, when we queried three of your top contact drivers last week.* That's a fact. It's not a slide. That's the only kind of evidence that survives a debate between headquarters and a strong-willed local entity.

And the throughline across all five dimensions is one movement: from **Answerability** (the customer has to read the page and act on it themselves) to **Actionability** (the system resolves the issue autonomously). Every serious CRM investment eventually points at that axis. Every serious *AI* investment does, too — which is exactly why "Agentic Self-Service" is a dimension in its own right, not a footnote.

## Why I keep working on this

The same measurement discipline that surfaced the twelve-country gap is what enterprise AI adoption needs next. Not another PoC. Not another platform layer. A rubric everyone can agree on, evidence live-audited from the customer's actual experience, and a portfolio-level view of where each business unit really stands — because that portfolio view is the only thing that funds the next move.

You can't argue with a scorecard grounded in what the customer actually sees. You can argue with everything else.
